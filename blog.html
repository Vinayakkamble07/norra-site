Build a research workflow that actually sticks

Good research is less about collecting everything and more about creating a reliable path from curiosity to clarity. Start by capturing sources at the moment you find them, while the context is still fresh.

Keep one notebook for each meaningful question, then use Norra to send conversations, articles, and reference pages directly into it. At the end of a session, ask NotebookLM to identify the strongest evidence, open questions, and the next action worth taking.

The result: your research becomes a trail you can revisit instead of a collection of tabs you have to rediscover.

Five prompts that make research summaries sharper

A useful summary should do more than shorten a document. Ask NotebookLM to separate established facts from assumptions, compare conflicting sources, and call out what the sources do not answer.

For a practical close, try: "Summarize this for a decision-maker. Include the recommendation, the evidence behind it, the main risk, and one question that still needs an answer."

Save the prompts that consistently produce useful results in your prompt library. A strong template saves time and makes your best thinking repeatable.

Why your best ideas are hiding in open tabs

Open tabs are often a map of unfinished thinking: a quote to revisit, a comparison you have not made, or a question that has not found a home yet. The problem is not a lack of ideas. It is a lack of retrieval.

Before closing a research session, capture the pages that changed your mind and add a short note about why they matter. That small habit gives every source a purpose and makes future synthesis dramatically easier.

When your sources live together, connections become visible. That is where scattered browsing turns into a knowledge system.
