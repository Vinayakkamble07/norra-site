Five prompts that make research summaries sharper

From the Norra desk

A useful summary should do more than shorten a document. Ask NotebookLM to separate established facts from assumptions, compare conflicting sources, and call out what the sources do not answer.

1. Separate facts from assumptions

"List the claims in these sources. For each, say whether it is a confirmed finding, an author's assumption, or an open question."

2. Surface conflicts

"Where do these sources disagree? Summarize each conflicting position and what might explain the difference."

3. Find the gaps

"What important questions do these sources not answer? What would I still need to look up?"

4. One-paragraph synthesis

"In one paragraph, what is the strongest conclusion I can draw from these sources combined?"

5. The decision-maker summary

"Summarize this for a decision-maker. Include the recommendation, the evidence behind it, the main risk, and one question that still needs an answer."
