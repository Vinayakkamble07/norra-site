Why your best ideas are hiding in open tabs

From the Norra desk

Open tabs are often a map of unfinished thinking: a quote to revisit, a comparison you have not made, or a question that has not found a home yet. The problem is not a lack of ideas. It is a lack of retrieval.

Before closing a research session, capture the pages that changed your mind and add a short note about why they matter. That small habit gives every source a purpose and makes future synthesis dramatically easier.

When your sources live together, connections become visible. That is where scattered browsing turns into a knowledge system.

The tab problem is really a timing problem. The insight is available exactly once — when you first encounter the source and your brain is still reasoning about why it matters. Capture at that moment and you keep the insight. Close the tab without capturing and the connection dissolves. You might re-find the article, but you will not re-find the thought.

Norra's web capture lets you send any page to a NotebookLM notebook in one click. You can add a note at capture time — "this contradicts the earlier study" or "good definition of X" — and that note travels with the source into your notebook.

The end-of-session habit: before you close your browser for the day, take two minutes to capture the three pages that mattered most. You do not need to capture everything — just the pages that shifted your thinking. Done consistently, this practice transforms a browser history into a personal knowledge base.
